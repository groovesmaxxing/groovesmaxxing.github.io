Mau P

🚧 bumpy amsterdam tech house

Maurits Westveen spent years making big-room edm under a different name. then the world shut down, he rebuilt from scratch, and came back as Mau P with a record about drugs from amsterdam that went straight to number one on beatport.

the students now became the teacher.

on the switch, 2022

both parents were musicians. his father a saxophonist, conductor and arranger, his mother a singer. his dad died when he was eighteen. he still makes records in his father's studio.

what followed: "Metro" with Kevin de Vries on Afterlife, the most-played track at tomorrowland in 2023. a residency at the Wynn in vegas. baddest behaviour, his own show, past 25,000 people across sold-out 2024 dates and now a second straight run of wednesdays at Pacha Ibiza. dj mag top 100 at 91 in 2024, then 77 in 2025.

for 2026 he has promised an album, a label and a clothing line. none of it is out yet.

Michael Bibi

🚧 bumpy london tech house

london, wimbledon specifically. his father is Robin Bibi, a blues guitarist who has played with Robert Plant and Ben E. King, so the music was in the house long before it was a job.

he co-founded Solid Grooves Records with PAWSA in 2015, though the party had been running a couple of years by then. "Hanging Tree" on Repopulate Mars in 2018 is the one that broke him wide. tech house at the dj awards in ibiza the year after. then SG Raw, then ISOLAT, then ISOLATE, his own all-night concept that came out of a meditation retreat in jamaica.

in may 2023 he was diagnosed with CNS lymphoma. he told everyone himself, the same week.

I don't know what lies ahead, I'm tired but I know I am strong & I won't let this beat me.

5 june 2023

four months later he walked into DC-10 for the solid grooves closing party and played a surprise set, days after finishing his main treatment.

When I first came into hospital I was barely able to walk. Today I walk out with a smile having completed my main treatment.

28 september 2023

he announced remission that december. in april 2024 he played coachella at sunset. that july he filled a 45,000-capacity show at finsbury park and called it One Life, for the city that raised him and treated him, then gave £124,832 of it to the royal marsden cancer charity. he did it again in 2025 and gave another £113,943. at his two-year scans in november 2025 he was still in remission.

Today was a good day. Thank you everyone that helped get us here and danced along the way.

14 november 2025

his line for all of it is one life, live it. that one is his, not ours.
